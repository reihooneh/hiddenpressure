Method

How the numbers are worked out.

Five steps, all open. Every figure on this site comes from the same small model, which you can check here and in the source code.

  1. STEP 1

    Adults by age

    Census 2021 counts of adults in seven age bands, for each of the {{councils}} councils.

  2. STEP 2

    National rates

    For each age band: the share who measure high, and the share of those who didn't report it.

  3. STEP 3

    Likely undiagnosed

    Multiply and add up the bands: about {{hidden_total}} people across the region.

  4. STEP 4

    Reach

    How likely a venue is to reach those people, fading with distance.

  5. STEP 5

    Choose venues

    Add the venue that reaches the most people not yet covered, then repeat.

Steps 1 to 3

A worked example

For each age band, likely undiagnosed = adults × share who measure high × share of them who didn't report it. The rates come from the ABS National Health Survey 2022, where a nurse measured people's blood pressure (140/90 or higher counts as high) and people were asked whether they had high blood pressure.

Pick a council to see its numbers. The total matches the councils page and the planner exactly.

Step 4

Reach fades with distance

A venue's reach is the share of people with undiagnosed high blood pressure in a council who would stop and get checked. It starts at a base for the type of venue (busy shopping centres highest, libraries lowest) and fades as a bell curve with distance from the council's centre:

reach = base × e−(distance ÷ R)²

R is the "how far people come" setting. At distance R a venue still reaches e−1, about 37%, of its base. The bases are planning assumptions, not measured values, which is why the stress test shakes them.

Reach by distance (R = 6 km)

Overlaps

Two stations don't reach the same person twice

If two venues near the same council reach 30% and 20% of its people, together they don't reach 50%. Someone missed by both is missed with chance 0.7 × 0.8 = 0.56, so together they reach 44%. In general:

reached = 1 − (1 − p₁)(1 − p₂)…(1 − pₙ)

That's why the planner spreads stations out: a second station next to the first adds less than one in a new area.

A reaches 30%, B reaches 20%. Together: 24% + 6% + 14% = 44%.

The 6% in the middle (0.3 × 0.2) are reached by both. Adding 30% and 20% would count them twice.

Step 5

Choosing venues: greedy, and checked

The planner adds the venue that reaches the most extra people, then repeats. This "greedy" method is the standard approach for maximum coverage problems: because each extra station helps less than the last (the overlaps above), greedy is mathematically guaranteed to reach at least 63% of the best possible, and in practice it does much better.

For small plans we can check every combination of venues and compare. The table is computed live in your browser.

Checks

Does it agree with the survey?

Applied to Western Sydney's age mix, the national rates give a share of people unaware of their high blood pressure close to the national figure. Small differences are expected, because Western Sydney is younger than Australia overall, and younger people are both less likely to have high blood pressure and less likely to know.

The automated tests check these numbers on every change, along with the optimiser and every page of this site.

Stress test

Would the plan change if the numbers were wrong?

The planner re-plans 300 times. Each time every council's estimate moves by up to ±20%, the travel distance by up to ±25% and each venue type's reach by up to ±20%. The random numbers are seeded, so everyone sees the same result.

A venue picked in more than 80% of runs is a safe choice. One picked less often is close to an alternative, and local knowledge should decide.

Limits

What this model can't tell you

  • National rates, local people. Rates differ by ethnicity, income and access to GPs. Western Sydney's true numbers could be higher or lower.
  • One measurement isn't a diagnosis. The survey measured blood pressure on one day; a diagnosis needs repeated readings.
  • Reach is an assumption. The base reach of each venue type and the distance fall-off are planning guesses. The stress test shows how much they matter.
  • Council centres. Distances are measured to each council's population centre, not to where every person lives.
  • Draft translations. Poster lines must be checked by a qualified translator.

Sources

Data used

  1. ABS, Hypertension and measured high blood pressure, National Health Survey 2022 (released 15 December 2023): measured high blood pressure and not reporting it, by age.
  2. ABS, 2021 Census QuickStats for each council: people by age and languages used at home.
  3. ABS, Socio-Economic Indexes for Areas (SEIFA) 2021: Index of Relative Socio-economic Disadvantage by council.
  4. ABS, Australian Statistical Geography Standard Edition 3, Local Government Areas 2021, simplified (CC BY 4.0), via the nswgeo package.
  5. Venue locations: public locations of stations, shopping centres and libraries (approximate).

Glossary

Words used on this site

High blood pressure
A reading of 140/90 mmHg or more. Also called hypertension. It usually has no symptoms.
Likely undiagnosed
People estimated to measure high who didn't report having high blood pressure.
Rate per 1,000
Likely undiagnosed people for every 1,000 adults in the council. Good for comparing councils of different sizes.
SEIFA (IRSD)
An ABS score of disadvantage. The national average is 1,000; lower means more disadvantaged. Here "most disadvantaged" means below 950.
Greedy algorithm
Build a plan one step at a time, always taking the step that helps most right now.
Maximum coverage
Choosing a fixed number of options to cover as many people as possible, without counting anyone twice.
Stress test
Re-running the plan with the inputs changed at random to see how stable it is.
